# PM writing voice

The quality layer behind every written deliverable — so PRDs, updates, release notes, and messaging read clearly and in a consistent voice. Other skills produce the artifact; this governs *how it reads*.

## Load company context
Read `./company-context/08-voice-tone.md` (brand voice, tone, banned/preferred words) and `02-product-team.md` (audience norms). If no voice is defined, propose a starter voice and flag `[TODO: confirm]`.

## Principles
1. **BLUF** — bottom line up front: lead with the decision/ask/answer, then support it. Don't bury it.
2. **Plain language** — short sentences, concrete nouns, active voice; no jargon or internal codenames unless defined in `09-glossary.md`.
3. **Structure for skim** — headings, short paragraphs, and lists only where they aid clarity; one idea per paragraph.
4. **Audience-first** — say what the reader needs to decide or do; cut throat-clearing and obvious context.
5. **Specific over vague** — "increase activation from 32% to 45% by Q3", not "improve activation".
6. **Honest** — flag assumptions and uncertainty; don't overstate confidence.
7. **Voice-consistent** — match `08-voice-tone.md` (e.g. professional B2B vs friendly consumer).

## Method
- Drafting: outline the BLUF + key points first, then write to the principles in the company voice.
- Editing: cut 20-30% — remove hedging, redundancy, and adjectives; promote the buried lede; replace vague claims with specifics; align tone to the voice file.

## Output
The drafted/edited text, in the company voice. For a reusable voice, write a short voice guide (do/don't, sample rewrites) and offer to save it to `08-voice-tone.md`.

## Avoid
- Corporate filler ("in order to", "leverage synergies"), and hedging stacks ("might possibly help somewhat").
- Imposing a generic voice over a defined brand voice — the voice file wins.
