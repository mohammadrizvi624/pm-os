# Porter's Five Forces

Evaluates the structural attractiveness of an industry — what determines profitability — across five forces. Industry-structure analysis, distinct from a head-to-head competitor teardown.

## Inputs
- The industry/market definition and the competitive landscape. Read attached material; research the industry if needed.

## Load company context
Read `./company-context/04-market-competitors.md` (market, segments, competitors).

## Method
Rate each force High/Medium/Low and note its trend:
1. Competitive rivalry — high when many similar rivals, slow growth, low differentiation, high fixed/exit costs.
2. Supplier power — high when few/concentrated suppliers, high switching costs, critical/unique inputs.
3. Buyer power — high when few large buyers, low switching costs, undifferentiated product, price-sensitive.
4. Threat of substitutes — high when accessible alternatives with good price/performance and low switching cost.
5. Threat of new entrants — high when low entry barriers, attractive margins, weak network effects/scale.
Then judge overall attractiveness, prioritize the 2-3 most critical forces, and define responses (reduce high-power forces, exploit weak ones, build defensibility).

## Output
Save to `./outputs/<YYYY-MM-DD>_five-forces_<slug>.md`: each force rated + trend + key dynamics, overall attractiveness, and strategic responses for the critical forces.

## Avoid
- Calling an industry "attractive/unattractive" in the abstract — position matters; the same industry differs by player.
- Treating it as static — re-assess as the market evolves.
