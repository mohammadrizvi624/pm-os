# Value proposition

Articulates the value delivered, customer-first, using a 6-part JTBD template (Huryn / Aatir Abdul Rauf) that ends in a reusable statement. One segment per pass.

## Inputs
- The product/feature and the target segment. Read attached customer insights or competitor material first.

## Load company context
Read `./company-context/03-users-personas.md`, `01-product.md`, `04-market-competitors.md`.

## Method — the 6 parts (one segment at a time)
1. Who — which segment, their characteristics and constraints.
2. Why — their core problem / job-to-be-done / desired outcome.
3. What before — their current situation and the friction in it.
4. How — how the product solves it; the specific capabilities that deliver value.
5. What after — the improved outcome; what becomes possible.
6. Alternatives — what they'd use instead, and why we're better (name the substitutes).
Then distil a 1-2 sentence value-proposition statement.

## Output
Save to `./outputs/<YYYY-MM-DD>_value-prop_<segment>.md`:
```
Who: …  | Why (JTBD): …
What before: …  →  How: …  →  What after: …
Alternatives: …  (why us)
Value-prop statement: <1-2 sentences>
```
Example (Canva): Who = non-designers making marketing graphics; Why = need professional designs without hiring a designer; Before = PowerPoint/Photoshop or costly freelancers; How = drag-and-drop templates + AI assist; After = pro designs in minutes; Alternatives = Photoshop (complex), Fiverr (slow/costly).

## Notes / Avoid
- Use the VPC (Strategyzer pains/gains) instead only when you need a detailed decomposition for a mature, complex product.
- Different segments get different value props — don't average them into one.
- This produces the value prop itself; turning it into campaign messaging/positioning is `pm-marketing`.
