# Positioning

Frames how the product should be perceived relative to alternatives — the competitive angle that the messaging and GTM then build on. Strategy's `value-proposition` defines the value; this positions it in the market.

## Inputs
- The product, target segment, and competitive context. Read attached files first; use web research on competitors where helpful.

## Load company context
Read `./company-context/04-market-competitors.md`, `01-product.md`, `03-users-personas.md`, `08-voice-tone.md`. Reuse any `value-proposition` output from `./outputs/`.

## Method
1. Map the competitive landscape — top ~5 competitors: their positioning angle, target focus, differentiators, and the **gaps they leave open**.
2. Generate ~5 distinct positioning ideas for the target segment, each: clearly differentiated, resonant with the segment's values, leaning on capabilities rivals downplay, and claiming an unowned territory.
3. For each, write: a one-line **positioning statement** ("For [segment] who [need], [product] is the [category] that [differentiated value] — unlike [alternative], we [why]"), the strategic rationale, supporting messages, and the competitive advantage that makes the claim credible.
4. Recommend the strongest, with why.

## Output
Save to `./outputs/<YYYY-MM-DD>_positioning_<slug>.md`: the competitive landscape, the ~5 ideas (statement · rationale · support · advantage), and a recommendation. Hand the chosen angle to `messaging`.

## Avoid
- A positioning claim the product can't actually back up.
- "Better/faster/cheaper" with no specific, ownable differentiation.
